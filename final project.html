What is a Stepwell?

Stepwells (baori / bawdi) are ancient rainwater-harvesting structures. They combine architecture and engineering to store monsoon water and provide cool shelter.

  • Built 600–1200 years ago in many regions of Rajasthan
  • Multi-storey steps reach down to the water table
  • Act as social hubs and ritual spaces

Engineering Magic

Stepwells work like natural air-conditioners. As you descend, temperature drops and stored rainwater is conserved for months. They also supported aquifer recharge.

Life Around the Stepwell

More than water — stepwells were meeting places, ritual sites, cooling shelters and child play spaces.

Decline & Revival

With piped water and urbanisation, many stepwells were abandoned, filled or neglected. Recently, NGOs and heritage groups have led restorations in several towns.

  • Some used as dumping grounds — sadly common
  • Restoration can return them to community use
  • They are climate-resilient water assets
